\section{Introduction}

Operations of the NSF-DOE Vera C. Rubin Observatory includes significant contributions from international collaborators. These collaborators are formed into in-kind prgrams with multiple contributions typical for most programs. In all, there are 28 counries (including the US and Chile), 44 in-kind programs, and approximately 150 contributions to the Rubin $''$science enterprise$''$. Of these contributions a small number of these are critical to the operation of Rubin itself. The larger number provide the science community with more resources to exploit Rubin's data products. Such contributions include telescope time for follow$-$up of Rubin ttransient and variable alerts, access to proprietary data sets, and many software programing resources for science analysis or other support. 

The smaller number of critical operations contributions support Rubin Data Management. Specifically, data release processing in the United Kingdom and France, and long haul, high bandwidth network paths through Brazil along the route from Chile to the United States.

These contributions are briefly described in the Rubin Operations Plan \citep{RDO-018}.
